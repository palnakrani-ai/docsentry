# Meridian Outfitters Hiring and Onboarding

This document covers how Meridian recruits, selects, and onboards. It applies to corporate, retail, and distribution center roles, with the differences noted.

## Opening a Role

Every role requires an approved requisition before any candidate is contacted. The requisition names the hiring manager, the pay band, the location, and whether the role is on-site, hybrid, or remote-eligible. Remote-eligible roles require VP approval, matching the Employee Handbook's remote work rules.

Job postings state the pay band range. Meridian posts ranges on every requisition regardless of whether the location requires it, on the reasoning that a candidate who learns the range at offer stage has wasted their time and ours.

## Selection

Every candidate for a given role receives the same interview structure and the same core questions. Interviewers score independently before discussing, because a group that discusses first converges on the first opinion voiced.

Corporate roles run a screen, a hiring manager interview, a skills exercise, and a panel. The skills exercise is time-boxed and paid where it exceeds 2 hours. Meridian does not use unpaid multi-day take-home exercises.

Retail roles run a screen and a store interview. Distribution center roles run a screen, a site visit, and a physical requirements review conducted by an occupational health provider rather than by the hiring manager.

Interviewers complete bias awareness training before joining a panel. Panels of a single demographic are flagged by the recruiting system and require a documented reason to proceed.

## Background Checks

Offers are contingent on a background check appropriate to the role. Corporate roles receive an identity and employment verification. Roles with financial authority additionally receive a credit check. Roles with facility access receive a criminal record check.

Meridian does not ask for salary history at any stage. Where a candidate volunteers it, the recruiter does not record it and does not pass it to the hiring manager.

A criminal record does not automatically disqualify a candidate. Where a record is returned, the relevance to the specific role is assessed, the candidate is given the chance to explain, and the decision and reasoning are documented.

## Offers

Offers state base pay, bonus eligibility, the pay band the role sits in, and the start date. The offer is issued in writing and no verbal offer is binding.

Initial offers are made at a level supported by the candidate's assessed skills against the band, not at the bottom of the band as an opening position. Meridian negotiates, but does not open low as a tactic.

Offers are open for 5 business days. Extensions are granted on request and are not held against the candidate.

## Pre-Start

IT provisioning begins once the offer is signed. Equipment ships to arrive at least 3 business days before the start date for remote and hybrid employees.

Accounts are created but disabled until the start date. A new hire has no system access before their first day, including for reading documentation, because access before employment begins creates both a security and a wage-hour problem.

## First Week

Day one covers payroll setup, benefits enrollment, the Code of Conduct, the IT Acceptable Use Policy, and a site safety induction where applicable. Distribution center hires complete the full safety induction before entering the floor, without exception.

Every new hire is assigned an onboarding partner, who is a peer rather than the manager. The partner's role is to answer the questions people are reluctant to ask a manager in the first month.

Managers hold a 30-minute check-in at the end of week one and weekly thereafter for the first 90 days.

## Probation and Early Departure

The first 90 days are a mutual assessment period. Managers give explicit written feedback at 30, 60, and 90 days. A concern raised for the first time at day 85 is a management failure rather than a performance finding.

New hires may not take paid leave during their first 60 days except in emergencies approved by HR, as stated in the Employee Handbook. Leave accrues from day one.

Where an employee leaves within 90 days, HR conducts a short review of what the hiring process missed. That review looks at the process and is not attached to the individual's record or to the hiring manager's performance file.

## Internal Movement

Employees may apply for any internal role after 12 months in their current role, or earlier with their manager's support. Managers may not block an internal application, and an employee is not required to tell their manager before applying, though HR encourages it.

Internal candidates receive a decision within 10 business days of their final interview, which is a shorter commitment than for external candidates because an internal candidate continues working alongside the outcome.
