# Meridian Outfitters Store Operations Manual

This manual covers daily operations at the 12 retail locations. It is written for store managers and shift leads.

## Opening and Closing

Stores open at 10:00 and close at 20:00 Monday through Saturday, and open 11:00 to 18:00 on Sunday. Holiday hours are published by the regional manager at least 21 days ahead.

Opening requires two employees on site before the doors unlock. The opening checklist covers alarm disarm, safe count, register float verification, fitting room check, and a walk of the sales floor for overnight damage.

Closing requires two employees to remain until the building is secured. No employee closes alone, which is a safety rule rather than a loss prevention one and is not waived for convenience.

The register float is $200 per till, counted at open and at close. A variance above $20 in either direction is reported to the regional manager the same day with the till, the shift, and the employees who used it. Variances are tracked rather than immediately treated as theft, because the common cause is a miscounted float.

## Cash Handling

Cash is dropped to the safe whenever a till exceeds $500. Drops are logged with time, amount, and employee.

The safe is opened by two people together for the daily deposit preparation. Single-person safe access is disabled at all locations.

Deposits are collected by the armored carrier on a schedule that is deliberately not published to staff beyond the manager, and the schedule is varied. Employees should not transport deposits personally under any circumstances, including when a pickup is missed. A missed pickup means the deposit stays in the safe.

## Returns at the Register

Retail returns follow the Returns and Refunds Policy. Store associates process returns within policy without needing approval.

A manager override is required for: a return without a receipt above $75, a return outside the stated window, a return of a final sale item, and any refund to a different payment method than the original.

Associates should not tell a customer that a return is impossible when it requires an override. The correct response is that it needs a manager, followed by getting one. Refusing on an associate's own authority is the most common source of escalations.

## Price Matching in Store

Associates approve price matches up to $75 of difference. Above that requires a store manager, as stated in the Price Match and Promotions Policy. Associates verify the competitor price on the competitor's live website, not from a screenshot, because screenshots are trivially edited.

## Fitting Rooms

Fitting rooms are checked every 30 minutes during open hours. Item counts in and out are tracked for anyone taking more than 6 items.

Employees do not enter an occupied fitting room. Where a loss prevention concern exists, the employee waits outside and involves a manager. This holds regardless of how strong the suspicion is.

## Staffing and Breaks

Employees working more than 5 hours receive a 30-minute unpaid meal break. Employees working more than 4 hours receive a paid 15-minute rest break, and a second one after 8 hours.

Breaks are scheduled, not taken ad hoc, and the schedule is posted at the start of the shift. A break missed because the floor was busy is a scheduling failure by the shift lead and must be reported, not absorbed.

Minimum floor coverage is two employees at all times. A store that cannot meet that closes rather than operating with one person present.

## Inventory

Cycle counts run weekly on a rotating section basis, so that every section is counted at least monthly. A full physical inventory runs twice a year, in February and August.

Shrink above 1.5% of sales for a location triggers a review by the regional manager and loss prevention. The review looks at process before it looks at people, since the most common causes are receiving errors and unrecorded damage.

Damaged goods are logged, marked, and routed to the recycling program described in the Sustainability and Materials document. Taking damaged goods home is theft under the Code of Conduct, regardless of whether the item was destined for recycling.

## Escalation

Store managers escalate to the regional manager for anything involving injury, a police matter, a media inquiry, or a customer threatening legal action. Employees do not speak to media, and the correct response is to take a contact and pass it to the communications lead.

Incidents involving customer data are escalated under the Incident Response Plan rather than through the retail chain, because those are time-bound in a way retail escalations are not.
