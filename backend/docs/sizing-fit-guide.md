# Meridian Outfitters Sizing and Fit Guide

This guide covers how Meridian sizes its products and how the fit names relate to measurements. The Product FAQ answers common sizing questions at a high level. This document is the detailed reference support uses.

## Fit Names

Meridian uses four fit names across apparel, and they are consistent between men's and women's lines.

**Next-to-skin** is cut close with minimal ease, intended to be worn against the body. Base layers use this fit. Sizing down is not recommended, since compression is not the intent.

**Trim** allows a thin base layer underneath. Most mid-layer fleece and lightweight insulated pieces use this fit.

**Standard** allows a base layer and a mid-layer underneath. Most shells use this fit.

**Expedition** allows a base layer, a mid-layer, and an insulated layer underneath. Heavy shells and belay parkas use this fit, and it appears oversized when worn alone, which is correct rather than a sizing error.

A customer who wears a Standard shell over nothing but a shirt will find it roomy. That is the fit working as designed, and support should explain the layering intent before treating it as a size problem.

## Measurement Basis

Meridian apparel is sized on body measurements rather than garment measurements. A size medium means the garment fits a body with a 39 to 41 inch chest, not that the garment measures 40 inches.

Size charts on each product page give the body measurement range. Garment measurements are available on request from support and are provided in a table covering chest, sleeve, and length.

Measure the chest at the fullest point with arms down, the waist at the natural waist rather than at the belt line, and the inseam from crotch to floor without shoes.

## Between Sizes

The Product FAQ states the general rule: size up for insulated layers and size down for base layers. The reasoning is that insulation loses loft when compressed, and a base layer that is loose does not wick.

For shells, size to the layers you intend to wear underneath rather than to your body. A customer who never wears more than a fleece under a shell should size to that, not to the maximum the fit allows.

For packs, size to torso length rather than to height. Torso length is measured from the C7 vertebra to the top of the iliac crest, and two people of the same height frequently take different pack sizes.

## Footwear

Meridian does not manufacture footwear and carries it only from partner brands. Sizing follows each brand's own standard and Meridian does not normalize across brands.

Footwear runs a half size small across most carried brands, with two exceptions noted on the product pages. Support should recommend ordering the customer's usual size and the half size up together, returning one, rather than guessing. Both items ship free and the return is free, so this costs the customer nothing but time.

Footwear cannot be repaired under the Product Care and Repair policy, which covers only what Meridian manufactures plus the pre-2019 exception.

## Inclusive Sizing

Meridian apparel is offered in XS through 3XL across the core range, and XS through 2XL in seasonal and limited pieces. The commitment is core range parity, meaning any piece Meridian carries year over year is offered in the full range.

Extended sizes are graded from a separate block rather than scaled up from the base pattern. Scaling a pattern up produces garments that fit no one, since bodies do not scale uniformly.

Extended sizes carry the same price as base sizes. Meridian does not surcharge for larger sizes.

## Returns for Fit

Fit returns follow the standard Returns and Refunds Policy. Fit is the most common return reason and is not treated as a problem to reduce through friction.

Where a customer has returned the same item in two sizes, support offers a fit consultation by video or in store rather than suggesting a third size blind. That consultation is free and takes about 15 minutes.

Items returned worn outdoors are assessed under the Returns and Refunds Policy. A garment tried on indoors is returnable. A garment worn on a trip is not, and support explains that distinction directly rather than declining without a reason.
