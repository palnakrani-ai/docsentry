# Meridian Outfitters IT Acceptable Use Policy

This policy governs how employees use Meridian systems, devices, and networks. It complements the Information Security Policy, which covers controls Meridian applies. This document covers what employees are expected to do.

## Covered Systems

The policy covers company laptops and phones, the corporate network at Denver and Portland, retail point-of-sale terminals, Meridian accounts on third-party services, and any personal device used to access Meridian email or documents.

Personal devices are in scope only for the Meridian data on them. Meridian does not inspect personal photographs, messages, or browsing on a personal device, and the mobile management profile is configured so that a remote wipe removes the Meridian container and nothing else.

## Accounts and Authentication

Every employee has one named account. Shared accounts are prohibited except for four documented service accounts in the distribution center, each with a named owner and a quarterly access review.

Multi-factor authentication is required on all accounts. Hardware keys are issued to anyone with administrative access, to finance, and to anyone handling customer payment data. SMS is not an accepted second factor for those roles, because SIM swap attacks defeat it.

Passwords must be unique to Meridian and not reused from any personal account. Meridian provides a password manager and expects it to be used. Password rotation on a fixed schedule is not required, which reflects current guidance that forced rotation produces weaker passwords rather than stronger ones.

Credentials must never be shared, including with a manager, including with IT. IT will never ask for a password, and any request for one should be reported as a phishing attempt regardless of who appears to be asking.

## Acceptable Personal Use

Incidental personal use of Meridian systems is permitted. Checking personal email, reading news, and making personal calls on a company phone are all acceptable in reasonable measure.

Personal use becomes unacceptable when it consumes work time to the detriment of the role, when it consumes significant network or storage resources, or when it exposes Meridian to legal or reputational risk.

Employees should not assume privacy in anything stored on Meridian systems. Meridian does not routinely monitor content, but it does retain logs, and it will access an account's contents where there is a legitimate investigative, legal, or operational need. Access of that kind requires approval from both the head of IT and HR, and is logged.

## Prohibited Use

The following are prohibited on Meridian systems:

- Installing software from outside the approved catalog without IT review
- Disabling endpoint protection, disk encryption, or logging
- Connecting unapproved devices to the corporate network, including personal routers and wireless access points
- Using Meridian systems to run a personal business
- Accessing, storing, or distributing unlawful material
- Attempting to bypass access controls, including on systems the employee is authorized to use
- Sending customer data to a personal account or personal storage service for any reason

The last item is absolute. Employees who need customer data outside a Meridian system should ask IT for a sanctioned path, and one exists for every legitimate case IT has been asked about.

## Artificial Intelligence Tools

Employees may use approved AI assistants for drafting, summarizing, and code assistance. The approved list is maintained by IT and currently includes three vendors under contracts that prohibit training on Meridian data.

Customer personal data, unreleased product information, supplier pricing, and anything covered by an NDA must not be entered into any AI tool, approved or otherwise. An approved contract limits how a vendor may use data. It does not make disclosure appropriate.

Output from an AI tool is the employee's responsibility. Code, policy text, and customer communications drafted with assistance are reviewed the same as anything else, and "the tool wrote it" is not an explanation for an error that ships.

## Departure

Access is revoked at the end of the last working day, and immediately in an involuntary separation. Managers are responsible for notifying IT of a departure date at least 3 business days ahead.

Company devices are returned within 5 business days of the last day. Data on a returned device is preserved for 90 days before the device is wiped and reissued, which allows a manager to recover work product.

Employees may not take copies of Meridian documents, code, customer lists, or supplier terms on departure. This includes material the employee personally authored.
