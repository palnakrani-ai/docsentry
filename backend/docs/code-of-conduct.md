# Meridian Outfitters Code of Conduct

This code applies to all employees, contractors, and temporary staff at the Denver headquarters, the Portland distribution center, and all 12 retail locations. It sits alongside the Employee Handbook, which covers leave, remote work, and expenses.

## Scope

The code applies during work hours, at any company-sponsored event, on any company system, and in any setting where an employee is identifiable as representing Meridian. It does not govern private conduct unrelated to work, with the exception of conduct that creates a credible safety risk to a colleague.

## Expected Conduct

Employees are expected to treat colleagues, customers, and vendors with respect, to raise concerns directly and early, and to accept correction without retaliating against the person who raised it.

Disagreement is expected and welcome. The distinction the code draws is between challenging an idea and demeaning a person. Persistent hostility toward a colleague is a conduct issue regardless of whether any single instance would be.

## Prohibited Conduct

The following result in investigation and may result in termination:

- Harassment or discrimination based on any protected characteristic
- Threats, intimidation, or physical aggression
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern or participates in an investigation
- Theft, including unauthorized markdowns, discount abuse, and removal of damaged goods designated for recycling
- Falsifying records, including time records, inventory counts, safety inspections, and expense reports
- Being impaired by alcohol or drugs while on duty or operating equipment
- Accessing customer data without a business reason, which is covered in more detail in the Data Retention and Privacy document

## Conflicts of Interest

Employees must disclose any outside employment, consulting, or ownership interest in a competitor, supplier, or vendor. Disclosure goes to HR and does not automatically prohibit the arrangement. Most disclosed conflicts are managed rather than forbidden.

Employees may not accept gifts from vendors valued above $50, and may not accept cash or cash equivalents of any amount. Meals and event tickets in the ordinary course of business are acceptable up to $150 per occasion and must be reported if they exceed that.

Hiring or directly managing a family member or partner requires written approval from HR before the arrangement begins. An undisclosed reporting relationship of this kind is a conduct issue on its own, separate from any question of favoritism.

## Reporting

Concerns may be raised with a manager, with HR, or through the anonymous ethics line, which is operated by a third party and available at all hours. Anonymous reports are investigated with the same seriousness as named ones, though anonymity can limit how far an investigation can go.

Meridian does not require employees to report a concern to their own manager first. Where the manager is the subject of the concern, that requirement would be self-defeating.

## Investigations

HR acknowledges a report within 2 business days and aims to complete an investigation within 30 calendar days. Complex investigations may take longer, and the reporter is told when that happens along with the reason.

The reporter is informed of the outcome in general terms. Specific discipline applied to another employee is not disclosed, because that is that person's private employment information.

Employees are expected to cooperate with investigations. Refusing to participate is itself a conduct matter. Employees may bring a support person to any investigatory meeting.

## Consequences

Outcomes range from coaching through written warning, final warning, and termination. The code does not require progressive discipline, and serious misconduct may result in termination on a first occurrence.

Terminations for conduct are reviewed by a second HR partner before they take effect, which is a control against a single manager acting alone on a disputed account.

## Retaliation

Retaliation is treated as seriously as the underlying conduct and is grounds for termination on its own. This holds even where the original report is not substantiated, because a report made in good faith that turns out to be mistaken is not misconduct.

A report known by the reporter to be false is a different matter and is itself a conduct violation.
