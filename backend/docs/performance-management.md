# Meridian Outfitters Performance Management

This document covers goal setting, review cycles, promotion, and the process where performance falls short. Pay decisions are covered in the Compensation and Benefits document, and conduct matters are covered in the Code of Conduct. Performance and conduct are handled separately and should not be conflated.

## Goals

Goals are set within the first 30 days of the calendar year, or within 30 days of a hire or role change. Each employee holds between 3 and 5 goals. Fewer than 3 usually means the role is under-scoped, and more than 5 usually means nothing is prioritized.

Goals state an outcome rather than an activity. "Reduce support first response time to 4 business hours" is a goal. "Work on response times" is not.

Goals are revised mid-year where circumstances change. A goal that has become impossible for reasons outside the employee's control is retired rather than carried forward as a failure.

## Review Cycle

There is one formal review per year, in January, covering the prior calendar year. It informs the pay decision made in the same cycle.

There is a lightweight mid-year check-in in July with no rating and no pay implication. Its purpose is to surface problems while there is still time to act on them.

Managers hold one-to-ones at least every two weeks. Feedback is expected continuously. Nothing in a formal review should be new information to the employee, and a surprise at review time is a management failure rather than an employee one.

## Ratings

Four ratings are used: Exceptional, Strong, Solid, and Below Expectations.

Solid means the employee did the job they were hired to do, well. It is the expected rating for most people in most years and is not a criticism. Meridian states this explicitly because organizations that treat the middle rating as a warning end up with inflated ratings and meaningless distinctions.

There is no forced distribution. Managers are not required to place a set percentage into any rating, and calibration exists to check consistency of standard rather than to enforce a curve.

## Calibration

Managers within a function review proposed ratings together before they are finalized. The purpose is to check that the same standard is applied across teams, since one manager's Strong should mean roughly what another's does.

Calibration discussions consider evidence rather than impressions. A rating that cannot be supported with specific examples is sent back.

HR reviews calibration outcomes for patterns by demographic group and raises anything anomalous before ratings are released.

## Promotion

Promotion recognizes that an employee is already operating at the next level, rather than predicting they could. Meridian does not promote on potential alone, because the failure mode is promoting someone into a role they then have to grow into without support.

Promotions are decided in the January cycle and in an off-cycle review in July. A manager may propose a promotion at any time, though the decision is made in one of the two windows.

Promotion requires a written case covering scope, sustained performance at the higher level, and the business need for the role. The case is reviewed by the manager's manager and by HR.

Promotion carries a pay increase to at least the minimum of the new band, and normally further into the band than the minimum where the employee has been operating at the level for some time.

## Below Expectations

A Below Expectations rating triggers a performance improvement plan. The plan states specific gaps, specific expectations, the support Meridian will provide, and a review date normally 60 to 90 days out.

The plan must be achievable. A plan an employee could not meet even with full effort is not a plan, it is documentation for a decision already made, and HR rejects plans of that kind.

Employees on a plan may bring a support person to review meetings and may request a different reviewer where they believe the assessment is not being made fairly.

Outcomes are successful completion, extension where progress is real but incomplete, role change where a different role is a better fit, or separation.

## Appeals

An employee may appeal a rating or a plan outcome to HR within 10 business days. The appeal is reviewed by an HR partner who was not involved in the original decision.

Appeals succeed where the process was not followed, where the evidence does not support the conclusion, or where the standard applied differs from that applied to peers. Disagreement with a fairly reached judgment is not on its own grounds for a changed rating, and HR says so plainly rather than leaving the employee to guess.
