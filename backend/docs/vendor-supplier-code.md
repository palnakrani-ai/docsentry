# Meridian Outfitters Supplier Code of Conduct

This code applies to every supplier, subcontractor, and licensed manufacturer producing goods for Meridian Outfitters. Acceptance is a condition of doing business and is incorporated into every purchase agreement.

## Labor Standards

Suppliers must not use forced, bonded, indentured, or prison labor. Workers must be free to leave employment with reasonable notice and must retain possession of their own identity documents. Holding a worker's passport is a terminating breach with no cure period.

The minimum working age is 16, or the local legal minimum where it is higher. Workers under 18 do not perform night shifts or hazardous work.

Wages must meet the legal minimum or the prevailing industry wage, whichever is higher, and must be paid on time and in full. Deductions as a disciplinary measure are prohibited. Recruitment fees must never be charged to workers, and where a labor agent has charged them, the supplier reimburses the worker.

Standard working hours must not exceed 48 per week, with overtime voluntary and capped at 12 additional hours. Workers receive at least one day off in every seven.

Workers have the right to organize and bargain collectively. Where local law restricts this, suppliers must not obstruct parallel means of independent worker representation.

## Health and Safety

Suppliers provide a safe workplace, including functioning fire detection, unobstructed and unlocked emergency exits, potable water, clean sanitation, and appropriate protective equipment at no cost to the worker.

Buildings must hold current structural and fire safety certification. Meridian requires documentary evidence at onboarding and at every renewal, not merely an assertion.

Dormitories, where provided, are separate from production and storage areas and meet the same fire safety standards.

## Environment

Suppliers comply with applicable environmental law and hold current permits for discharge, emissions, and waste. Wastewater from wet processing is treated before discharge, and test results are provided to Meridian quarterly.

Restricted substances are listed in the Meridian Restricted Substances List, which is updated annually and is stricter than legal requirements in several categories, notably PFAS in durable water repellent finishes. Suppliers were required to eliminate intentionally added PFAS from all Meridian production by the end of 2024. Related material commitments appear in the Sustainability and Materials document.

## Ethics

Bribery, kickbacks, and facilitation payments are prohibited in every form. Suppliers must not offer gifts above $50 to any Meridian employee, matching the limit in the Meridian Code of Conduct, and must never offer cash.

Suppliers disclose any family or financial relationship between their personnel and any Meridian employee. Non-disclosure of such a relationship is treated as a serious breach even where the relationship itself would have been acceptable.

Subcontracting any part of Meridian production requires written approval in advance. Unauthorized subcontracting is a serious breach, because it moves production to a facility Meridian has not assessed.

## Audits

Meridian audits every finished-goods facility at least every 24 months, and annually for facilities rated at elevated risk. Audits are conducted by an accredited third party.

At least 30% of audits each year are unannounced. Suppliers grant access to production areas, dormitories where applicable, and payroll and hours records. Refusing access ends the relationship.

Workers are interviewed privately, off site where the auditor judges it necessary, and without management present. Retaliation against a worker who speaks to an auditor is a terminating breach.

## Findings and Remediation

Findings are graded critical, major, or minor.

Critical findings include forced labor, child labor, blocked fire exits, falsified records, and retaliation. Production is suspended immediately and the supplier is placed on notice of termination. Meridian does not simply exit, since abrupt exit harms the workers the standard exists to protect. Meridian requires a remediation plan and monitors it, and terminates where the supplier will not act.

Major findings require a corrective action plan within 15 business days and closure within 90 days. Minor findings close within 180 days.

Falsifying records to pass an audit is treated as a critical finding regardless of what the underlying condition was, because a supplier that falsifies cannot be assessed at all.

## Transparency

Meridian publishes its tier one supplier list annually, including facility name, city, and worker count. Tier two disclosure is in progress and covers fabric mills first.

Suppliers may raise concerns about Meridian's own conduct, including unrealistic lead times or pricing that makes compliance impossible, through the same anonymous ethics line available to Meridian employees. Purchasing practices that make the labor standards unachievable are treated as a Meridian problem to fix.
