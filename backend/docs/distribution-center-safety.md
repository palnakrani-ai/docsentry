# Portland Distribution Center Safety Manual

This manual applies to all staff, temporary workers, and visitors at the Portland distribution center. Safety rules here are not discretionary and are not traded against throughput.

## Personal Protective Equipment

Steel-toed footwear is required anywhere on the warehouse floor, including for visitors and including for a walk-through of under a minute. Loaner footwear is stocked at reception in sizes 5 through 15.

High-visibility vests are required in the receiving and shipping bays and anywhere powered equipment operates. Cut-resistant gloves are required when opening cartons, which is where the majority of recordable injuries at this site originate.

Hearing protection is required in the sorter aisle during operation. Eye protection is required in the maintenance shop and when using compressed air.

## Powered Equipment

Forklifts, reach trucks, and order pickers are operated only by certified staff. Certification is renewed every 3 years, and after any incident regardless of fault.

Pedestrians have right of way at all times. Operators sound the horn at every blind corner and at every aisle entry. Speed inside the building is capped at walking pace in pedestrian aisles and 5 mph in equipment lanes.

An operator who observes a pedestrian in the equipment lane stops and waits. Operators do not maneuver around pedestrians, because the pedestrian's next move is not predictable.

Equipment is inspected at the start of every shift using the checklist attached to the unit. A unit failing any checklist item is tagged out immediately and is not returned to service until maintenance signs it off. Removing a tag-out without maintenance sign-off is grounds for termination.

## Manual Handling

The single-person lift limit is 50 pounds. Loads above that require a two-person lift or mechanical assistance, and both are always available.

Oversized items such as kayaks and large tents are two-person handling regardless of weight, because the awkward geometry rather than the mass causes the injury.

Employees are not disciplined for asking for help with a lift, and supervisors who discourage it are subject to the Code of Conduct.

## Racking and Storage

Pallets are not stacked above the marked line on each rack upright. Damaged pallets are removed from service and not repaired on the floor.

Any rack impact, however minor, is reported immediately and the bay is cordoned until inspected. A rack that has been struck may have lost capacity in ways that are not visible, which is why the standard is report every impact rather than report visible damage.

Aisles and emergency exits are kept clear. Nothing is stored in front of an electrical panel, a fire extinguisher, an eyewash station, or an exit door, including temporarily during a peak shift.

## Emergency Procedures

Evacuation alarms mean leave immediately by the nearest exit and assemble at the marked point in the north lot. Do not stop to secure equipment or retrieve belongings. Supervisors take a headcount and report to the incident commander at the assembly point.

Fire extinguishers are for a small fire between you and your exit. Anything larger means evacuate and let the fire service handle it. No employee is expected to fight a fire.

The eyewash and safety shower stations are tested weekly and the test is logged. First aid kits are inspected monthly.

Serious injuries are reported to the site manager and to HR within 1 hour. Near misses are reported the same shift through the safety board. Near miss reporting is deliberately low friction, because near misses are the cheapest information the site gets about what is about to go wrong.

## Temperature and Peak Staffing

The building is not climate controlled beyond the office area. During heat advisories, work-rest cycles apply: 15 minutes of rest per hour above 90 degrees Fahrenheit and 20 minutes above 95, taken in the cooled break area, and hydration is provided.

During the November 15 to December 24 peak, temporary staff receive the same safety induction as permanent staff before their first shift, without exception. A temporary worker who has not completed induction does not enter the floor, and no supervisor has authority to waive this.

Shifts during peak are capped at 10 hours and no employee works more than 6 consecutive days. Fatigue is treated as a safety hazard rather than a scheduling inconvenience.

## Contractors and Visitors

Contractors sign in, complete a short induction, and are escorted until they have completed the full induction. Work involving hot work, work at height, or electrical isolation requires a permit issued by the site manager.

Visitors are escorted at all times and do not enter equipment lanes.
